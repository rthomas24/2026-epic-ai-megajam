## Purpose and story context

A colossal unidentified vessel-the Dominion-has appeared at the edge of the star system. Hostile machines emerging from it have begun probing planets and attacking patrols. Command dispatches the three-pilot Triad reconnaissance unit: Wren (Rift), Kato (Lancer), and Moss (Bastion). Their orders are to identify the vessel and report-not to start a war. Unbeknownst to the squad, the Dominion and its forces originate from their own future. That truth must remain hidden until the finale.
Opening promise: awe, unease, a little squad banter, then immediate danger. The player should experience the unidentified ship as a persistent mystery, not receive a lore lecture.

## Opening cinematic  |  Approx. 2 minutes 20 seconds


### 00:00-00:25  |  Missing patrol

Black screen; a patrol distress call breaks through static. Reveal a quiet star system and the distant silhouette of something impossibly large. Delay the musical score to emphasize scale and uncertainty.

> Command, we have an unidentified structure on the outer perimeter. It-s not on any chart. Wait-something-s coming out of--

### 00:25-00:55  |  Triad arrives

Rift, Lancer, and Bastion emerge from a jump and form up. Establish the pilots through short, recognizable exchanges, not exposition.
COMMAND
> Triad Squadron, investigate Patrol Seven-s last coordinates. Identify the contact and report. Recon only. Do not engage.-WREN
> Three ships, one mystery. How bad could it be?-KATO

### 00:55-01:30  |  The impossible vessel

The team approaches the Dominion. Its immense surface is seemingly dormant. Instruments briefly glitch; Kato detects an unfamiliar encrypted signal that resembles a military authentication handshake. It stops before he can resolve it.
WREN
> Okay. That is a big ship.-KATO
> No propulsion signature. No answer to our hails. Wait-we-re receiving something.-MOSS
> A message?-KATO
> More like a handshake. And now it-s gone.-

### 01:30-02:00  |  The first launch

Hangars open across the Dominion. Drones stream toward the planets, largely ignoring Triad. One drone scans Rift at close range, briefly changes its targeting light from red to blue, and holds fire. Then a separate group breaks formation and attacks.
WREN
> Why isn-t that one firing?-KATO
> Unknown. Multiple contacts are turning toward us.-COMMAND
> Triad, disengage. Repeat, disengage immediately.-

### 02:00-02:20  |  The interrupted warning

As the attack begins, an intensely distorted voice reaches the player over comms: -Don-t--. The signal cuts. Wren orders a break; the camera locks smoothly into the player-s playable ship view and the HUD activates.
UNKNOWN VOICE
> Don-t--WREN
> Triad, break formation!-COMMAND
> Pilot, incoming fire! Evade now!-

## Tutorial battle  |  Directly after the cut

- Evasion: learn basic flight movement while avoiding the first drone volley.
- Weapons: acquire targets and destroy a small, readable group of drones.
- Squad play: introduce switching among Rift, Lancer, and Bastion, then each ship-s signature ability (Cloak, EMP Pulse, Gravity Well).
- Escalation: a larger hostile craft arrives; the squad learns coordinated combat under pressure.
- Withdrawal: command orders a retreat. Drones cease pursuit at a boundary, suggesting they are guarding a task or territory rather than simply seeking kills.
Mission handoff: a distress report arrives from Verdance. Dominion machines have landed around a remote research facility and begun probing beneath the surface. Command redirects Triad to investigate. This links the battle directly to the first planetary mission.

## Finale payoff  |  Same event, opposite side of time

After boarding the Dominion, Triad discovers evidence connecting its systems, command codes, and fleet to their own future. A recovered communication record then leads into a short scene on the Dominion-s command deck, showing the exact opening transmission from future Triad-s perspective. Reuse the identical processed audio of -Don-t--; do not rerecord the fragment.

### Finale cutscene - Future command deck

Future Kato: -All three signatures confirmed. It-s us.-
Future Moss: -They-re exactly where we were.-
Future Wren: -Open a channel. Now.-
Future Kato: -Temporal interference. Less than a second.-
Future Wren: -Don-t--
The signal collapses. On a monitor, three younger fighters break formation as the drones attack-the exact moment the player witnessed at the start. Future Wren quietly acknowledges that it happened the same way again.

### The complete intended warning

-Don-t engage the Dominion. You don-t understand what happens if you do.- - Future Wren
The full warning is revealed through the recovered recording or surrounding dialogue, not by changing what the younger squad heard. Whether engaging the Dominion directly causes the future catastrophe is deliberately unresolved at this stage; the warning should create doubt before the final reactor confrontation, without claiming an unearned answer.

## Narrative continuity and production rules

- The Dominion is the source of the enemies seen throughout the campaign, but its motivation stays concealed until late in the story.
- Future Triad is not a conventional villain: they are attempting to prevent a catastrophic future and protect their younger selves, even when autonomous machines fight them.
- Present-day Triad must still have a compelling reason to board the ship and ultimately destroy its reactor. The moral uncertainty should heighten the final choice, not invalidate the player-s objectives.
- Keep the opening lean: in-engine ship cameras, radio performances, a launch sequence, a scan/drone hesitation, and one gameplay camera transition.
- Preserve the exact warning fragment and its audio treatment so the finale payoff is unmistakable. Avoid giving the audience clear time-travel terminology in the opening.
OPEN STORY DECISIONS  -  The specific future catastrophe, the true nature and mechanics of the Origin Core, and the causal relationship between attacking the Dominion and that catastrophe still require final decisions. These should be resolved before locking finale dialogue.
