**FBX (`SM_NullBomber.fbx`) — preferred for Unreal.** Exported with the repo's Unreal convention
(`apply_unit_scale=True`, `apply_scale_options='FBX_SCALE_UNITS'`, `axis_forward='-Z'`,
`axis_up='Y'`, face smoothing, tangents, triangulated). The file's `UnitScaleFactor` /
`OriginalUnitScaleFactor` are **100** (centimetres). Reimported in Blender it measures
11.800 × 10.346 × 2.156 m, centred on the origin. Import with the usual Interchange
pipeline, uniform scale **1.0**; it should measure the brief length in Unreal.

**GLB (`SM_NullBomber.glb`).** Vertex values are in **centimetres**, with `export_yup=false`, so the
file itself is +Z up / +X forward rather than glTF's default +Y up / +Z forward. glTF's official unit
is the metre: if an importer assumes metres, set uniform scale to **0.01**; if it treats the numbers as
cm (or you are in Unreal and want the brief length in uu), use scale **1.0**. The GLB embeds the OpenGL (glTF)
normal, plus BC, metallic-roughness and emissive. Prefer the FBX for the engine import; Blender's default
glTF importer will swap Y/Z if it applies a second +Y-up conversion on top of `export_yup=false`.

## Lead-note check: red-orange seams and the white 'eye' bar

Checked on the 2K maps and on the derived-mesh renders (see `contact_sheet.png`).

- **Red-orange seams: present.** They read on the dorsal chevrons, wing inner edges, engine rings and leading-edge trims, in both `texture_diffuse.png` / `T_NullBomber_BC.png` and `texture_emissive.png` / `T_NullBomber_E.png`. The emissive has about 71,100 orange pixels (1.70% of the 2K map). They are clearly visible in the front, top and 3/4 contact-sheet views. The hull is a dark, slightly warm grey (mean RGB about 35, 23, 22), close enough to black that the seams have good contrast.
- **White eye sensors: present, a little soft.** The emissive has about 2,400 near-white pixels (0.058% of the map). On the mesh they read as small pale / white lamps on the leading edge, in the same places as the concept, but they are softer and less disc-like than the concept's four hard white eyes.

## Notes and issues

- The silhouette is faithful: a wide manta / flying-wing hull, a pointed central nose, wingtip claws, three rear engine bells, and dorsal orange chevrons. It clearly shares the Null / Triad family language with the Fighter and Gunship (black armour, orange seams, white eyes, chevrons) as asked in R-034.
- The approved concept is followed closely. Small departures: the white eyes are softer than the concept; some leading-edge panel lines are a little less hard-edged, which is expected at 8.5k tris versus the 12–20k budget.
- **Flag — triangle budget:** 8,478 tris is under the 12–20k brief. The pack is a Clean Topology export; there is no higher-poly file in the download. It was not decimated (and could not be up-res'd here).
- No rig, no sockets, no UCX collision — please add those on import if you want them. This drop was not run through `blender-ai-mesh-cleanup`.
- Topology is Rodin's auto-triangulation (not clean quads).
- A per-ship heat-glow mask matching these UVs is in `../heatmask_r1/`.

## Normal convention

`source/texture_normal.png` (and the normal embedded in both the raw and the derived GLB) is
**OpenGL** (Y+): it is the glTF normal, B-channel is nearly 255 (outward Z), and the glTF 2.0 spec
requires OpenGL tangent-space normals. That matches how Rodin ships a glTF pack.

For Unreal, which expects **DirectX** (Y−), the delivery map `T_T_NullBomber_N.png` has the green
channel inverted (`G' = 1 - G`). Red and blue are unchanged. This is the same convention used for
the accepted Drone mesh_r1, the player-ship mesh_r1, the Warden mesh_r1 and the earlier Null fleet
meshes. When importing the loose maps, set N as a Normalmap (DirectX) and do **not** also use the
GLB-embedded OpenGL normal.
