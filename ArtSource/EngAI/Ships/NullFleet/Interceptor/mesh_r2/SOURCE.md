**FBX (`SM_NullInterceptor.fbx`) — preferred for Unreal.** Exported with the repo's Unreal convention
(`apply_unit_scale=True`, `apply_scale_options='FBX_SCALE_UNITS'`, `axis_forward='-Z'`,
`axis_up='Y'`, face smoothing, tangents, triangulated). The file's `UnitScaleFactor` /
`OriginalUnitScaleFactor` are **100** (centimetres). Reimported in Blender it measures
6.600 × 6.329 × 2.209 m, centred on the origin. Import with the usual Interchange
pipeline, uniform scale **1.0**; it should measure the brief length in Unreal.

**GLB (`SM_NullInterceptor.glb`).** Vertex values are in **centimetres**, with `export_yup=false`, so the
file itself is +Z up / +X forward rather than glTF's default +Y up / +Z forward. glTF's official unit
is the metre: if an importer assumes metres, set uniform scale to **0.01**; if it treats the numbers as
cm (or you are in Unreal and want the brief length in uu), use scale **1.0**. The GLB embeds the OpenGL (glTF)
normal, plus BC, metallic-roughness and emissive. Prefer the FBX for the engine import; Blender's default
glTF importer will swap Y/Z if it applies a second +Y-up conversion on top of `export_yup=false`.

## Lead-note check: red-orange seams and the white 'eye' bar

Checked on the 2K maps and on the derived-mesh renders (see `contact_sheet.png`).

- **Red-orange seams: present.** They read on the hooked wings, the dorsal chevron, the chin-gun trims and the engine rings, in both albedo and emissive. The emissive has about 33,400 orange pixels (0.80% of the 2K map). They are clearly visible in the front, top and 3/4 views. The hull is a deep black (mean RGB about 27, 17, 18), the darkest of this fleet drop, so the seams have good contrast.
- **White eye: present, but dim.** The emissive has about 6,360 near-white pixels (0.15% of the map), yet on the mesh the nose eye reads as a small, soft lamp — much dimmer and less disc-like than the concept's bright white eye, and weaker than the r1 clay silhouette suggested. This is the main colour / read flag on this ship.

## Notes and issues

- The hooked wings, chin guns, vertical tail and pointed nose still read as the same family as the Fighter and as the untextured `mesh_r1`. Black armour, orange seams and chevrons are on theme (R-034).
- **Flag — two rear engines vs one.** The approved concept (and the r1 clay mesh) has a single rear engine; this r2 mesh has two stacked engine bells.
- **Flag — eye and nose.** The white eye is much dimmer than the concept's bright disc, and the nose is a little blunter / less needle-like.
- **Flag — new mesh, not a texture on r1.** 8,589 tris and 660.0 × 632.9 × 220.9 cm, versus `mesh_r1` at 8,974 tris and 660.0 × 535.3 × 248.0 cm. Do not expect the r2 maps to fit the r1 UVs. Prefer this `mesh_r2` for the textured import.
- No rig, no sockets, no UCX collision — please add those on import if you want them. This drop was not run through `blender-ai-mesh-cleanup`.
- Topology is Rodin's auto-triangulation (not clean quads).
- A per-ship heat-glow mask matching these r2 UVs is in `../heatmask_r1/`.

## Normal convention

`source/texture_normal.png` (and the normal embedded in both the raw and the derived GLB) is
**OpenGL** (Y+): it is the glTF normal, B-channel is nearly 255 (outward Z), and the glTF 2.0 spec
requires OpenGL tangent-space normals. That matches how Rodin ships a glTF pack.

For Unreal, which expects **DirectX** (Y−), the delivery map `T_T_NullInterceptor_N.png` has the green
channel inverted (`G' = 1 - G`). Red and blue are unchanged. This is the same convention used for
the accepted Drone mesh_r1, the player-ship mesh_r1, the Warden mesh_r1 and the earlier Null fleet
meshes. When importing the loose maps, set N as a Normalmap (DirectX) and do **not** also use the
GLB-embedded OpenGL normal.
