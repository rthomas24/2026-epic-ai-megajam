## Scale and palette notes (on the boards, not generated)

- Player-ship scale is **Rift 5.00 m** (approved mesh extents in `ArtSource/EngAI/Ships/Rift/mesh_r1/SOURCE.md`). The art-direction brief's "~7 m" was not used.
- Region measurements are from `docs/eng/SPACE-DESIGN.md`: belt plane ±60 m and 8° tilt, band width 600 m (radius 2.0–2.6 km), moonlets 250–400 m, graveyard hulls 80–200 m, dish platform 15–25 m, survey rigs 30–60 m, hollowed rocks 40–120 m.
- A **180 cm figure is omitted** on every board: at these sizes it would not read (0.6% of a 300 m moonlet; about 1/50 of an 80 m hull). The boards say so in the scale panel.
- Palette swatches mix official sRGB values from `docs/visual/Art-Direction.md` and `.claude/skills/blender-game-asset/references/art-direction.md` (Rift violet `#6B2BD9` and magenta `#FF2BD6`, Verdance teal `#19C3B1` and `#0F3B3A` / `#3CFFE0`, Cinder basalt `#1A1718` / ash `#4A4446` / cyan crystal `#2EE6FF` / magma `#FF5A1F`, Null black `#0E0E12` / crimson `#C3122E` / white eye `#F4F7FF`, Verdance violet `#7B3FE4`) with belt / nebula / hull values chosen to match the key mood.

## Inspection notes

- **Shepherd Belt.** The mood keyframe reads as a true thin ring with a natural gap, size gradation from inner ochre rocks to outer gravel, and four rounder moonlets at the gap edges — not a piled clump. The silhouette makes the flat plane and the 5 m fighter against a ~300 m moonlet obvious. Apparent ship size in the pictures is a little large versus a strict 1:60 moonlet ratio; the measured bars on the board are the authority.
- **Quiet Graveyard.** Six long dark civilian hulls in a loose line, lit windows, spilled debris, buoys, a dish-platform beacon, no rocks, and Verdance as a teal world in the distance. Still, cold lighting. The silhouette is a 2.39:1 letterboxed camera; the board cover-fits it to 16:9.
- **Survey Scar.** Hollowed rocks with cut faces and bore holes, angular gloss-black Null rigs with crimson seams and white eyes, violet and cyan crystals, a rubble stream toward a volcanic Cinder, and the fighter in a clear channel. Industrial and searched-over, not a random rock pile.
